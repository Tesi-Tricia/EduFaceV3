Privacy and compliance checklist

Not legal advice. A practical checklist aligned to the principles of Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 (regulator: the Personal Data Protection Office, PDPO, under NITA-U) and good practice for children's biometric data. The school is the data controller. Have the school's lawyer or a data-protection adviser confirm current requirements, registration and deadlines with the PDPO. Outside Uganda, use the local law.

Why this is high-risk

Face data is biometric and belongs to children. A password can be changed; a face cannot. EduFace stores as little as possible, but the school's decisions decide whether use is lawful and fair.

Before go-live

Responsibility

  • ☐ A named data-protection officer / responsible person (head teacher, deputy or board member).
  • ☐ Checked whether the school must register with the PDPO; registered if required (keep the certificate).
  • ☐ Written agreements with anyone who handles the data (installer/support, whoever has administrator access).

Need and fairness (a one-page DPIA)

  • ☐ Written: what we do and why; why face recognition rather than cards/QR; risks; safeguards; who approved; review date.
  • ☐ Purpose limited to attendance and campus safety. Not used for discipline scoring, marketing, or shared with others.
  • ☐ A non-biometric alternative (QR card / manual) works for every student.

Consent for children

  • ☐ For each child: written, informed, voluntary consent from a parent/guardian for face-based attendance (separate from consent for messages).
  • ☐ The form says in plain language: what is collected (a numeric template of the face — not photos), why, who can see it, how long it is kept, where it is stored (this computer only, no cloud), the right to refuse without any penalty, the right to withdraw anytime, and who to contact.
  • ☐ Refusal does not disadvantage the child. ☐ Consent is recorded in EduFace before any face is captured (the app blocks enrolment without it) and the signed form is filed.
  • ☐ Parents know how to withdraw; withdrawal deletes the face data immediately.
  • ☐ Staff and visitors are informed; signs at the gate and visitor desk ("Face scanning in use — ask for the alternative").

Transparency

  • ☐ A privacy notice available to parents at admission and on request (data, purpose, retention, rights, how to complain to the school and the PDPO).

Rights requests and incidents

  • ☐ A named person handles requests to see, correct or delete a child's data and replies within the legal time. EduFace can show a student's attendance history; deleting face data is immediate (Enrol faces → Withdraw consent).
  • ☐ A one-page incident plan: who to call, how to disable accounts (Users page), who notifies the PDPO and affected parents. The Act expects prompt breach notification — confirm the deadline and form with the PDPO. Examples: stolen computer or backup USB, misused administrator account. A lost backup passphrase or backup file is an incident too.

Ongoing

  • Yearly: review consents, remove leavers, repeat the accuracy test, review the DPIA.
  • Monthly: audit-log check; test restoring a backup.
  • Before any new use of the data: update the notice and the DPIA.
